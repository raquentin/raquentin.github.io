Our interest in them is disproportionate, as any class bipartitions the universe, but few so violently.

Some constraints only become meaningful when everything is forced to appear at once.

Graphs do not inhabit space, yet when embedded into dimensions, some give particular dissonances.

The failure is combinatorial on the presence of $K_{3,3}$ or $K_{5}$ or similar. On $K_4$, invisible. On $K_{2,3}$, boring.

Planarity survives because it fails loudly. Two dimensions are not special; only unforgiving. Below them, structure trivializes. Above them, it hides.

Euler’s appears, separators shrink, duals emerge. None of these belong to the graph.

This preference is not mathematical. It is epistemic.

We return to objects that tolerate total exposure. We distrust those whose properties require routing, depth, or deferred interaction.

Planar graph theory records one such boundary. The boundary generalizes.
